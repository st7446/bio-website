Protein Data Bank & Databases

Global repositories of protein structures and sequences enable researchers worldwide to access, analyze, and build upon previous discoveries.

Major Protein Databases

🏛️ Protein Data Bank (PDB)

Repository of 3D structures of proteins, nucleic acids, and complexes. Founded 1971. >200,000 structures from X-ray crystallography, NMR, cryo-EM, and AlphaFold. Free public access at rcsb.org. Peer-reviewed curation process.

🤖 AlphaFold Database

AI-predicted 3D structures for 200+ million proteins. Covers most known proteins on Earth. Dramatically expanded structural coverage. High confidence predictions for ~47% of proteins. Freely accessible through ESMFold and AlphaFold2.

📚 UniProt (Universal Protein Resource)

Comprehensive resource combining sequence and functional information. >200 million protein sequences. Expert curation with Swiss-Prot and computational annotations with TrEMBL. Maps to PDB, GO terms, and other databases.

🔬 NCBI Protein Database

Protein sequences from GenBank annotations. Linked to genetic sequences and literature. MolWt, pI calculations. BLAST search for homologs. Over 500 million entries. Part of U.S. National Library of Medicine.

🧬 InterPro

Integrates protein families, domains, and functional sites. Combines PfamNanoScan, SMART, and others. Protein classification and function prediction. Annotations for genomic sequences. Essential for genome annotation.

🔗 STRING & BioGRID

STRING: Protein-protein interaction network predictions and experiments. BioGRID: Comprehensive interaction database. Curated from literature. Tools for network analysis and visualization. Essential for systems biology.

Analysis & Visualization Tools

🖥️ PyMOL

Most popular structure visualization software. Creates publication-quality images and animations. Scripting capabilities for automation. Free open-source and commercial versions. Standard in structural biology labs.

💻 BLAST (Sequence Alignment)

Fastest sequence homology search tool. Basic Local Alignment Search Tool. Finds similar sequences in databases. Estimates evolutionary distances. Web version at NCBI. Can run locally on custom databases.

🌐 Mol* 3D Viewer

Modern web-based 3D protein viewer. Default viewer for PDB website. Lightweight, fast, interactive. No software installation required. Includes structural analysis tools. Mobile device support.

🧮 Clustal Omega

Multiple sequence alignment tool. Aligns protein or DNA sequences. Reveals conservation patterns. Phylogenetic inference. Web server or downloadable version. EBI service with high accuracy.

🔍 Jmol

Open-source 3D molecular viewer. JavaScript-based for web. Loads PDB files directly. Protein sequence display linked to structure. Educational tool for teaching protein structure.

📊 DSSP & Secondary Structure

Defines secondary structure assignment. DSSP: hydrogen bond patterns determine structure. STRIDE & PSEA: alternative algorithms. Important for structure quality assessment and comparison.

PDB Growth & Milestones

1971: PDB Founded

First protein structure: myoglobin (1MBN). Single structure at Brookhaven National Laboratory. Manual data collection and curation. Marked beginning of structural biology as systematic science.

1990s: Growth Acceleration

Synchrotron radiation facilities improved. Automation of crystallography. PDB crosses 1,000 structures. Public internet access increases usage worldwide. Becomes essential resource for structural biology.

2000s: Genomics Era

Structural genomics initiatives target representative proteins. High-throughput crystallography developed. PDB reaches 50,000 structures. NMR and small-angle X-ray scattering data included.

2010s: Cryo-EM Revolution

Cryo-EM resolution improvements enable new structures. Large complexes now accessible. PDB reaches 150,000 structures. Integration with multiple data types. Recognition of cryo-EM breakthrough with 2017 Nobel Prize.

2020: AlphaFold Breakthrough

AI successfully predicts structures. AlphaFold2 achieves ~88% accuracy. Collaborative efforts predict entire proteomes. PDB+AF provides ~200M structures coverage. Transforms protein research.

2024: Present State

PDB >200,000 experimental structures. AF covers most proteins. Advanced ML methods continue improving predictions. Integration of diverse data types. Open science enables rapid discovery and drug design.
