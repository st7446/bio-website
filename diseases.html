Protein Diseases

When proteins misfold or are defective, serious diseases result. Understanding protein diseases helps develop treatments and reveals the critical importance of proper protein structure and function.

Protein Misfolding Diseases

🧠 Alzheimer's Disease

Characterized by accumulation of amyloid-beta (Aβ) plaques and tau tangles in the brain. Aβ peptides misfold and aggregate, forming insoluble plaques. Leads to neuronal death, memory loss, and dementia. Affects ~6 million Americans.

🤝 Parkinson's Disease

Results from misfolding of α-synuclein protein in substantia nigra dopaminergic neurons. Forms Lewy bodies (protein aggregates). Causes progressive loss of motor control, tremor, and rigidity. Affects ~1 million Americans.

👻 Prion Diseases

Rare but fatal neurodegenerative diseases caused by prions (misfolded PrP proteins). Normal PrPc converts to pathogenic PrPsc. Includes Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) in humans, mad cow disease in cattle. Always fatal.

💪 Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)

Progressive neurodegenerative disease affecting motor neurons. Caused by misfolding of SOD1 and other proteins. Leads to muscle weakness and paralysis. Stephen Hawking had ALS. Currently no cure.

🫁 Cystic Fibrosis

Mutations in CFTR protein (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator). ~90% of mutations cause protein misfolding. Misfolded CFTR is degraded before reaching the cell membrane, impairing chloride transport.

🫡 Polyglutamine Diseases

Huntington's disease and other disorders caused by abnormal expansion of glutamine repeats. Proteins become sticky and aggregate. Huntington's shows trinucleotide CAG repeat expansion (>40 repeats causes disease).

Genetic Protein Deficiencies

🩸 Sickle Cell Disease

Single amino acid substitution in β-globin (Glu6Val) causes polymerization and sickling of red blood cells. Resulted from natural selection in malaria-endemic regions. Causes painful crises, strokes, and organ damage.

🦴 Osteogenesis Imperfecta (Brittle Bone Disease)

Defective collagen I synthesis due to COL1A1/COL1A2 mutations. Collagen is primary structural protein in bone. Results in fragile bones that fracture easily. Severity ranges from mild to lethal.

🧬 Phenylketonuria (PKU)

Enzyme deficiency causing phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency. Cannot convert phenylalanine to tyrosine. Excess phenylalanine causes intellectual disability if untreated. Newborn screening allows early intervention.

🩸 Hemophilia

Blood clotting factor deficiency (Factor VIII or IX). X-linked inheritance. Causes excessive bleeding from minor injuries. Women are carriers, men show disease. Treatment with recombinant clotting factors.

🦴 Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)

Mutations in dystrophin gene cause muscle protein deficiency. Progressive muscle degeneration starting in childhood. Wheelchair-bound by teens. Primarily affects males. ~1 in 3,500 boys.

🔴 Hereditary Spherocytosis

Defects in red blood cell membrane proteins (spectrin, ankyrin). Abnormal cell shape leads to hemolysis (breakdown). Causes anemia, jaundice, and sometimes splenic enlargement.

Protein Aggregate Diseases

Amyloidosis

Accumulation of amyloid fibrils (misfolded protein aggregates) in tissues. Over 30 different proteins can form amyloid. Causes AL amyloidosis, AA amyloidosis, and others. Damages organs, especially heart and kidneys.

Aggregation-Prone Proteins

Some proteins naturally aggregate (lysozyme, transthyretin). Point mutations promote aggregation. Temperature and pH affect folding. Chaperone proteins normally prevent inappropriate aggregation.

Aggresomes

Compartments where misfolded proteins accumulate. Proteasome cannot degrade large aggregates. Autophagosomes engulf aggresomes for complete removal. Dysfunction contributes to neurodegenerative disease.

Protein Quality Control

Heat shock proteins (HSPs), proteasome, and autophagy maintain proteostasis. Decline with age contributes to age-related diseases. Enhancing protein quality control is a therapeutic target.

Therapeutic Approaches

Protein Replacement Therapy

Supply functional protein versions to replace defective ones. Examples: Factor VIII for hemophilia, pancreatic enzymes for cystic fibrosis. Challenges include protein stability, immunogenicity, and delivery to tissues.

Gene Therapy

Correct genetic defects at the DNA level. Introduce functional genes into patient cells. Examples: spinal muscular atrophy treatment (Zolgensma). Challenges include delivery and off-target effects.

Chemical Chaperones

Small molecules that promote protein folding or stabilize native structures. Example: miglustat for Gaucher disease. May slow misfolding without providing full cure.

Immunotherapy

Use antibodies to target specific proteins. Monoclonal antibodies against amyloid-beta show promise in Alzheimer's. Aducanumab and lecanemab are recent approvals. Better efficacy in early disease stages.

Proteasome Inhibitors

Prevent degradation of normal proteins while affecting cancer cells differently. Used in multiple myeloma. Bortezomib and carfilzomib are FDA approved.

Personalized Medicine

Genetic testing identifies disease-causing mutations. Allows targeted treatments. Pharmacogenomics optimizes drug choice based on genetic profile. Promise of precision medicine in protein diseases.
