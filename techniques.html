Experimental Techniques

Scientists use diverse techniques to study protein structure, function, and interactions. From crystallography to AI prediction, these tools have revolutionized protein biology.

Structural Determination Methods

💎 X-Ray Crystallography

Gold standard for protein structure determination. Proteins are crystallized and X-rays are diffracted to create diffraction patterns. Resolution achieves ~2 Å (angstrom). Provides highest-resolution atomic structures. ~90% of PDB structures from crystallography.

🧲 NMR Spectroscopy

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance determines 3D structures in solution (not crystals). Studies protein dynamics and flexibility. Typical resolution ~5 Å. Better for small proteins (<30 kDa). Reveals conformational changes and interactions.

❄️ Cryo-Electron Microscopy (Cryo-EM)

Revolutionized structural biology (Nobel Prize 2017). Proteins rapidly frozen in vitreous ice. Electron microscopy achieves near-atomic resolution (~3-4 Å). Can image large complexes. No crystallization needed. Rapidly growing technique.

🤖 AlphaFold AI

Machine learning predicts 3D structures from amino acid sequences. Achieved breakthrough accuracy in 2020. AlphaFold2 provides structures for 200+ million proteins. Democratizes structural biology. Doesn't require experimental structures.

🧬 Small-Angle X-Ray Scattering (SAXS)

Studies protein shape and size in solution. Scattering patterns reveal overall dimensions. Lower resolution than crystallography but faster. Useful for verifying structures and studying conformational dynamics.

🔬 Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)

Mechanically probes protein structures at atomic resolution. Can unfold proteins to study stability. Directly measures forces involved in protein interactions. Real-time imaging of protein dynamics.

Analytical & Functional Methods

⚖️ Mass Spectrometry (MS)

Measures molecular mass precisely. Protein identification through peptide mass fingerprinting. Detects post-translational modifications. Topdown MS for intact proteins; bottom-up MS after proteolytic digestion. High sensitivity and specificity.

🌈 Circular Dichroism (CD) Spectroscopy

Studies secondary structure composition. Different structures (α-helix, β-sheet) have characteristic CD spectra. Rapid method to assess protein fold. Monitors protein folding and denaturation. Measures changes upon ligand binding.

💡 Fluorescence Resonance Energy Transfer (FRET)

Measures distances between fluorophores on proteins. Studies protein interactions and conformational changes. Energy transfer occurs within ~50 Å. Real-time monitoring of protein dynamics. Applications in high-throughput screening.

🧫 SDS-PAGE & Western Blotting

Size separation of proteins by gel electrophoresis. SDS-PAGE denatures and separates by molecular weight. Western blotting detects specific proteins with antibodies. Standard technique for protein quantification and identification.

💧 Size Exclusion Chromatography

Separates proteins by size in solution. Determines molecular weight and oligomeric state. Studies protein-protein interactions. Purifies proteins based on size. Non-denaturing method preserves native structure.

🔗 Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR)

Real-time monitoring of protein interactions. Measures binding kinetics and affinity. Label-free detection. Studies ligand-binding, kinetics, and specificity. Applications in drug development and diagnostics.

Sequence & Interaction Analysis

💻 BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool)

Compares query sequences against databases to find homologs. Identifies related proteins in other organisms. Estimates evolutionary relationships. Most widely used bioinformatic tool. Essential for sequence analysis.

🧮 Clustal Omega & Multiple Sequence Alignment

Aligns multiple protein sequences to identify conserved regions. Reveals evolutionary relationships. Identifies functionally important residues. Guides mutagenesis studies and drug design.

🤝 Co-immunoprecipitation (CoIP)

Studies protein-protein interactions by pulling down antibody-bound protein complexes. Identifies binding partners. Western blot reveals co-precipitated proteins. Provides evidence for physical interactions.

📊 Yeast Two-Hybrid (Y2H)

Identifies protein-protein interactions in living cells. Transcription factor is split into two parts. Interaction of bait and prey proteins reconstitutes activity. High-throughput screening of interaction networks.

🔍 Microscale Thermophoresis (MST)

Studies molecular interactions and binding kinetics. Measures changes in protein hydration state upon binding. Label-free detection. Requires only nanoliters of sample. High sensitivity for weak interactions.

🐟 Crosslinking & Mass Spectrometry

Identifies protein-protein interaction sites. Chemical crosslinkers covalently link proximal residues. MS identifies crosslinked peptides. Reveals structural details of complexes. Applications in structural proteomics.

Emerging Technologies

Single-Molecule Studies

Studies individual protein molecules using optical/magnetic tweezers. Reveals heterogeneity and rare conformations. Measures unfolding forces and kinetics. Provides unique insights into protein dynamics unavailable from bulk studies.

Cryo-Electron Tomography (Cryo-ET)

3D reconstruction of cellular structures at near-atomic resolution. Studies proteins in cellular context. Reveals in situ organization of complexes. Bridges gap between isolated structures and cellular function.

AI-Driven Structure Prediction

AlphaFold and similar models predict structures from sequences. OmegaFold and others emerging. Enables rapid prediction of entire proteomes. Accelerates functional annotation. Opens new possibilities for design.

Deep Learning for Function

Neural networks predict protein function and interactions from sequences and structures. Learn patterns from data. Identify disease-associated variants. Enable computational drug discovery.

Microfluidics & Lab-on-a-Chip

Studies proteins in miniaturized systems. High-throughput screening of interactions. Reduced consumption of samples and reagents. Real-time monitoring of reactions. Enables new experimental designs.

Quantum Computing Applications

Simulating protein folding and dynamics using quantum computers. More efficient than classical computing for some problems. Still in early stages. Future potential for drug discovery and design.
